# Curriculum Fact-Check Source Register

This register records authoritative references used for targeted safety and health wording changes. It is **not** a claim that every factual statement in the 730-lesson curriculum has been independently verified.

## Verified references

| Topic | Source | What it supports | Applied to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Button/coin cell safety | [U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission — Button Cell and Coin Batteries](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Button-Cell-Coin-Battery-Information-Center) | Swallowed button/coin batteries can cause severe internal injury quickly; children should not handle loose cells; secure battery compartments; seek immediate medical attention after suspected ingestion. | Electricity projects now require adult installation/secure holders and prohibit child access to loose batteries or wall-socket experimentation. |

## Source-selection policy

For new or revised claims, prefer:

1. Government agencies, official statistical agencies and primary legal/regulatory sources.
2. Peer-reviewed research and recognised professional bodies.
3. Reputable museums, universities and scholarly publishers for history, culture and heritage.
4. Multiple independent sources for contested or uncertain claims.

For data that changes (population, prices, rankings, current policy, technology adoption), record the publication date and the date checked. Distinguish established evidence from estimates, interpretations, traditional narratives and religious beliefs. Do not convert a traditional account into a historical fact or present one community's practice as universal.

## Open review items

The full curriculum still needs qualified educator and subject-matter review for factual accuracy, developmental fit and cultural nuance. Automated validation and the targeted source checks above do not replace that review.
